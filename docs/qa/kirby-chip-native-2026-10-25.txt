Actual native life/order, and why the earlier hypothetical case is insufficient

Weapons run before the ordinary enemy loop. In a legal staged pit, a page1
Goomba starts at its native ground originY184, flag1/state0, ordinary horizontal
speed, zero vertical force. Missing ground sets falling state1 and the native
gravity kernel moves it. At age29 the native high-page write changes1->2,
lowY1. The next weapons entry sees high2/Y1/state1 at age30. The subsequent
EnemyGraphicsEngine -> SprObjectOffscrChk -> EraseEnemyObject removes it.
No fixture write ever sets an enemy high page other than initial1.

This proves a one-update off-page active window, not the old CPU fixture's
high0/2,lowY184 state. Those arbitrary high/low combinations were not reproduced
through this native life sequence. Defeated enemies also have state20 set and
are rejected before the ordinary contact path. Grounded inhale explicitly
requires page1 and moves lowY toward KirbyY+8; it clears vertical force and
does not manufacture a borrowed high page. This investigation did not exhaust
every flying enemy, room or knockback path.

Reachable Chip/Dale defect differs from the original page-only proposal

A legal upper brick platform supports Chip/Dale atY32 above the pit. A real
Up+B input releases a carried native crate atY24. Native crate physics moves
it toY12. Simultaneously, the same initially page1 Goomba has naturally fallen
toY244, still high1/state1. CdBoxEnemies compares only low bytes and accepts
the 240-pixel separation as a small wrapped difference. Thus a high-page==1
guard alone would not fix the observed defect.
